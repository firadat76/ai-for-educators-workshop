# Plagiarism & AI Detection (3:35–3:55)

A Turnitin AI score is a reason to look closer and talk to the student, never proof of misconduct; Turnitin's own guide says it "should not be used as the sole basis for adverse actions against a student" ([Turnitin](https://guides.ithenticate.com/hc/en-us/articles/27835303398541-AI-writing-detection-in-the-new-enhanced-Similarity-Report-view)). The strongest protection is assessment design, not detection.

### Two different numbers in one report

|  | Similarity score | AI writing indicator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it measures | Text that matches web pages, publications and past student papers | Prose that is likely AI-generated, or AI-generated then altered by a paraphraser or "humaniser" (bypasser) tool |
| How it shows | Percentage + coloured matches to sources | Separate percentage + highlights; independent of the similarity score |
| What a high number means | Copied or poorly cited text, or properly quoted material and common phrases | Writing patterns typical of AI; not proof of who wrote it |
| What you check | Open each match: is it quoted and cited? | Talk to the student; look at drafts and process |

### Facts to know (Turnitin guides, checked 19 Oct 2026)

| Point | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Languages | English, Spanish, Japanese. Urdu and Roman Urdu text is not assessed |
| Length | 300 to 30,000 words of long-form prose |
| File types | .docx, .pdf, .txt, .rtf, under 100 MB |
| Low scores | 1–19% shows as "\*%" with no highlights, because false positives are more likely in that range |
| Not reliable for | Bullet points, tables, outlines, code, poetry, very short answers |
| Bypasser detection | Flags text run through AI "humanisers"; announced Sept 2025, English only, needs the Turnitin Originality / AI add-on licence |
| Who sees it | Instructors; depends on IBA's licence and Sakai integration (confirm with CICT) |

### Limitations to say out loud

- **False positives happen.** Turnitin's "under 1%" false-positive figure applies to documents with 20% or more AI-flagged text, not to every paper.
- **Some human writing looks like AI.** Formal, formulaic or very concise prose, and writing by multilingual students, can score higher. Many IBA students write in their second language.
- **Edited AI text can slip through,** and students who used AI for ideas but wrote themselves may score 0%.
- **Permitted AI use looks the same as misuse.** Grammar tools and AI you allowed can raise the score.
- **The score covers only qualifying prose,** so it may not reflect the whole document.

### When a score is high: three steps

1. **Read the work, not the number.** Look for a sudden change in voice, generic content, wrong facts, or citations that don't exist or don't say what is claimed.
2. **Ask for process evidence.** Drafts, notes, Word or OneDrive version history, sources used. Invite the student to talk you through the work.
3. **Have a conversation, not an accusation.** "Walk me through how you wrote section 2" is better than "Turnitin says you used AI." Follow IBA's academic integrity procedure if concerns remain, with several kinds of evidence.

**Never:** fail a student on the AI score alone, paste student work into free online "AI detectors" (privacy and accuracy problems), or treat 0% as proof of honest work.

### Designing assignments that resist AI misuse

- **Make it local and current:** a Karachi business, this month's SBP decision, data students collect themselves.
- **Make it personal:** the student's own decision, experience or field observation, with justification.
- **Grade the process:** proposal → draft → final, with feedback in between; include a short process reflection.
- **Add a live component:** a 5-minute viva, in-class presentation or whiteboard defence of the submitted work.
- **Use class-specific material:** a case discussed in class, a guest speaker's point, the course's own dataset.
- **Allow AI openly where it helps,** and ask students to submit their prompts and say what they changed.
- **Use the rubric:** reward reasoning, evidence and local insight; generic prose earns little.

### Sample AI-use statement for your course outline

Copy and adapt one line per assessment:

```text
AI use in this course
- RED (no AI): in-class quizzes, mid-term and final exams.
- AMBER (AI for support only): assignments and projects. You may use AI to brainstorm, explain concepts or check grammar. You may not use it to write, solve or analyse for you. Add a short note: which tool, what for, and what you changed.
- GREEN (AI encouraged): [named activity], where using and critiquing AI output is part of the task.
Undisclosed AI use is treated under IBA's academic integrity policy. A Turnitin AI score alone is never treated as proof.
```

### Table discussion: scenario cards (8 min)

Each table takes one card, agrees what to do in 4 minutes, and one table reports back.

1. A strong student's essay shows 68% AI. Her earlier work is similar in style, and she says she used Grammarly heavily.
2. A group report shows 0% AI, but three references don't exist.
3. A student's take-home answer is 12% (shown as "\*%") but reads nothing like his in-class writing.
4. Your colleague wants to fail every submission above 30% AI "to send a message". What do you say?

**Debrief line:** "In every case the next step was the same: look at the work, ask for the process, talk to the student. Detection starts the conversation. Design prevents the problem."
