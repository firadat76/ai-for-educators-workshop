# Opening script (0:00–0:20)

Read the first 10 minutes close to word for word; the second 10 minutes is a guided mindset exercise. Stage directions are in brackets.

**0:00 — Welcome (2 min)**

"Assalam-o-Alaikum, and good afternoon, everyone. Thank you for giving four hours of a working Tuesday. I'm Faisal Iradat, and on behalf of IBA-CICT and SMCS, welcome to *AI for Educators: Future-Ready Teaching*.

Let me start with a promise. By the time you leave this room, you will have built something real: an AI tutor for one of your own courses, one your students could use tonight. Not a demo. Yours.

Some of you use AI every day; some of you have barely opened ChatGPT. Both are fine. If you are new, you will not be left behind. If you are experienced, I'll ask you to help a colleague, and you'll learn something by doing it."

**0:02 — Why this matters now (3 min)**

"Our students are already using AI. Some use it well. Many use it to skip the thinking we are trying to teach. We can't stop that by banning it, and we can't catch all of it with a detector — we'll see why later today.

So today's question is: *how do we make AI work for learning, instead of against it?*

My answer is the Socratic method. A good tutor doesn't hand you the answer. A good tutor asks the question that gets you one step closer. That is what we build today.

&#91;Click: 'AI should guide students, not do the work for them.'\]

If you remember one sentence from today, make it this one."

**0:05 — What you'll leave with (2 min)**

"You will leave with five things.

One: a working AI tutor for your course in Microsoft Copilot Studio, grounded in your own course outline.

Two: two or three teaching materials of your choice — an assignment with a rubric, a quiz, lecture slides, a case study, a role-play, or a student survey.

Three: a clear, honest picture of what Turnitin's AI detector can and cannot tell you.

Four: the steps to put your tutor inside your Sakai course.

Five: a 30-day plan, written by you."

**0:07 — How today works (2 min)**

"I'll show you a finished tutor I built for CSE 455, Computer Security. Then you build your own. After the break you'll make teaching materials, then we'll talk about detection and Sakai.

Three housekeeping points. Everything — every template and prompt — is in the shared folder. \[Point to QR.\] Scan it now. These colleagues \[name helpers\] are our table helpers; if you're stuck, hold up the red card. And please don't upload anything with student names, grades or unreleased exam papers today."

**0:09 — Know the room (1 min)**

"Quick show of hands. Who has never used AI for teaching? \[Pause.\] Sometimes? Every week? \[Pause.\] If you're in the first group, there is a weekly user near you — they're your partner for the next hour."

**0:10–0:20 — Mindset shift exercise (10 min)**

1. **Two-minute confession (pairs).** "Tell your neighbour one thing you worry AI is doing to your students' learning, and one boring task you'd happily hand to AI." Collect three of each aloud.
2. **From worry to design (3 min).** Put the worries on the left of the board and turn each into a design rule on the right. Example: "They copy answers" becomes "The tutor never gives graded answers." "It makes things up" becomes "It only uses my outline and says when it doesn't know."
3. **Three principles slide (3 min).**
   - *Socratic by design:* the tutor asks before it tells.
   - *Integrity by design:* it never completes graded work, and assessments are built so AI alone can't finish them.
   - *You stay the teacher:* every AI output is a draft you check.
4. **Bridge (2 min).** "Those rules you just wrote are the instructions we'll give the AI in an hour. Let me show you what a student sees."
