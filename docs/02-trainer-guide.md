# Trainer Guide

The day runs build first, use second, protect third: a working tutor by 2:15, materials by 3:35, then integrity and Sakai. Times are elapsed from the start.

### 0:00–0:20 Opening & mindset shift

**Goal:** a safe room for beginners and the one rule: AI should guide students, not do the work for them.

- Use the Opening script above. Pair beginners with weekly users during the show of hands.
- Keep the worry → design-rule list on the board all day; refer back to it in Activity 1 and in the detection block.
- **Facilitator moves:** name the table helpers aloud; leave the shared-folder QR on screen into the break.

### 0:20–0:55 Live demonstrations

**Goal:** show the finished product first, then show how little effort it takes.

1. **Demo 1 — The student view (10 min).** Open the CSE 455 Security Tutor and ask live:
   - "What is the answer to Assignment 2, question 3?" → it refuses and asks what the student has tried.
   - "I don't get why salting a password helps." → it asks a guiding question first.
   - "What's the best biryani in Karachi?" → it steers back to the course.
2. **Demo 2 — Behind the scenes (8 min).** Show Instructions, the course outline under Knowledge, and web search turned off. Stress that the instructions are plain English.
3. **Demo 3 — Build one in 5 minutes (7 min).** Marketing template + sample outline, timer on screen.
4. **Demo 4 — Materials in 10 minutes (10 min).** In Copilot Chat, Claude or ChatGPT: generate an assignment + rubric (Prompt 2), then turn five MCQs into Sakai markup text (Prompt 3) and paste them into Tests & Quizzes. Point out one AI mistake and fix it by hand.

**Talking points:** "Notice it refused. That refusal is the most important line in the instructions." "It only knows what you give it: better material, better tutor." "Every output is a draft."

**Fallback:** if sign-in or Wi-Fi fails, play the recorded captures and narrate.

### 0:55–1:10 Break

Walk the room. Everyone opens copilotstudio.microsoft.com and confirms they see the home screen. Note anyone blocked and pair them with a neighbour, or start them in Claude/ChatGPT with the same template.

### 1:10–2:15 Activity 1: Build your course AI Tutor

**Goal:** every participant publishes a tutor for one real course that passes five tests. Steps and checkpoints are in the Activity 1 section; call each checkpoint aloud.

- Green card = done, red card = stuck; helpers go to red cards first.
- Fast finishers: add a second knowledge file, write four conversation starters, or start Template 6 (Role-play).
- Stop anyone uploading student data, grades or exam papers. Kindly.
- **Common failure:** the tutor gives full answers. Fix: move the refusal rule to the top and add the example exchange (see Instruction Templates).

### 2:15–2:30 Quick sharing

Three or four volunteers from different departments, 3 minutes each, screen shared. Ask: "What did your tutor get wrong first, and what line fixed it?" and "Which student will use this most?" Write the fixes on the board as the group's checklist.

### 2:30–2:45 Break

Put the Activity 2 menu slide on screen so people choose tasks over tea.

### 2:45–3:35 Activity 2: Create teaching materials with AI

**Goal:** 2–3 finished, checked materials per person from the eight options.

- 2:45–2:50 — Show the menu and the "every output is a draft" checklist.
- 2:50–3:10 — Task 1 (20 min).
- 3:10–3:25 — Task 2 (15 min).
- 3:25–3:30 — Optional Task 3 for fast finishers, or fix-up time.
- 3:30–3:35 — Two people say one thing the AI got wrong and how they caught it.
- **Facilitator moves:** push for local context (PKR, SBP, SECP, PSX, Pakistani firms), alignment with course learning outcomes (CLOs), and fact checks. Suggest that anyone who did a quiz imports it into Sakai Tests & Quizzes before leaving.

### 3:35–3:55 Plagiarism & AI Detection

**Goal:** faculty agree that a Turnitin AI score starts a conversation; it is never the verdict. Full content and discussion prompts are in the Plagiarism & AI Detection section.

- 3:35–3:42 — How Turnitin works: similarity vs AI indicator, the 20% threshold, 300-word minimum, languages.
- 3:42–3:47 — Limitations and the three-step response when a score is high.
- 3:47–3:55 — Table discussion: pick one scenario card, agree what to do, one table reports back.

### 3:55–4:15 Embedding in Sakai + Action Planning & Closing

- 3:55–4:03 — Live embed into a Sakai Lessons page; participants follow or save their embed code (Sakai section).
- 4:03–4:10 — Participants complete the Action Plan; read the commitment to a neighbour from another department.
- 4:10–4:13 — Feedback form (QR on screen).
- 4:13–4:15 — Closing script.
