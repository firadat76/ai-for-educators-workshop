# Diagnostic quiz: EdTech awareness check-in (10 minutes)

Built in Curipod as "EdTech Check-in: AI Tools Workshop (10 min)". The questions below match the Curipod lesson as revised on 22 Oct 2026:

- Slide 1: a poll with no correct answer.
- Slides 2–11: 10 multiple-choice questions, 50 seconds each.
- Each correct answer has an explanation that students see after answering.

The same questions are in EdTech_Checkin_Questions.csv, and there is a Poll Everywhere version in PollEverywhere_AI_Prompt.md.

**Levels (questions 2–11):** 0–4 Explorer · 5–7 Practitioner · 8–10 Advanced.

**Answer key:** 1 (poll; intended B) · 2 C · 3 B · 4 A · 5 D · 6 B · 7 C · 8 A · 9 B · 10 D · 11 C

**1. Warm-up poll (not scored).** The core design rule emphasised in the workshop is?
- A. "AI should do as much of the student's work as possible so they finish faster."
- B. (intended) "AI should guide students, not do the work for them."
- C. "Students must never be allowed to see or use any AI tool."
- D. "Faculty should only use AI for administrative tasks, never for teaching."

*Why:* This is the one rule for the day (opening slide "The one rule for today").

**2. Adoption.** A colleague says only a minority of students really use AI. In the Digital Education Council's 2024 global survey (3,839 students, 16 countries), what share used ChatGPT?
- A. 25%
- B. 45%
- C. ✅ 66%
- D. 9%

*Why:* ChatGPT comes first in every survey that names tools. Grammarly and Copilot were 25% each, and 95% of UK undergraduates use AI in some form (HEPI 2026). (Slide: What students use)

**3. Adoption.** Harvard's Faculty of Arts and Sciences gave students ChatGPT Edu, then ended open access after June 2026. Why?
- A. A data breach
- B. ✅ Low undergraduate uptake, and cost
- C. A US ban on AI in coursework
- D. It moved everyone to a local model

*Why:* Harvard cited cost and undergraduate uptake far lower than it expected. A licence alone doesn't change what students use. (Slide: What students use)

**4. HEC.** A strong student's essay gets a 68% AI score in Turnitin. Under HEC's August 2026 draft AI policy, what should you do first?
- A. ✅ Read it, ask for drafts, talk to the student
- B. Fail it: 68% is above any threshold
- C. Confirm with two more free detectors
- D. Ignore it: detectors are always wrong

*Why:* HEC's draft rules out any penalty based on an AI-detection report alone. It also asks every university for an AI policy and an AI committee, student disclosure of AI use, and AI literacy within two years. Yale, Vanderbilt and Johns Hopkins switched Turnitin's AI detector off. (Slides: Pakistan, national moves; Four shared patterns)

**5. GDPR.** VU Amsterdam does not license ChatGPT, and teachers there may not require students to use it. Why?
- A. It costs too much per student
- B. Dutch law bans AI in teaching
- C. Students prefer Copilot
- D. ✅ No data-processing agreement, which GDPR requires

*Why:* Under GDPR (Article 28), a university that lets a supplier process personal data needs a contract with that supplier. That is why European universities favour licensed tools or their own platforms. (Slide: Wider Europe)

**6. HAWKI.** About 30 German universities used HAWKI in 2024, and Baden-Württemberg's bwGPT runs on it. What is HAWKI?
- A. A German AI-detection tool
- B. ✅ A self-hosted, open-source AI chat interface
- C. A paid Copilot add-on
- D. A Sakai grading plugin

*Why:* It was first built at HAWK Hildesheim and gives privacy-first access to AI models. Germany's data-protection rules push universities towards shared or self-run platforms such as KI:connect.nrw, HAWKI and GWDG Chat AI. (Slide: Germany)

**7. Assessment design.** A colleague wants to stop students from simply pasting the assignment into ChatGPT. Which assessment design strategy is most aligned with current best practice?
- A. Ban all AI tools and rely only on Turnitin's AI score.
- B. Make every assignment multiple-choice so AI cannot help.
- C. ✅ Redesign the task so students must show process, oral defence, etc.
- D. Only accept handwritten submissions.

*Why:* Detectors are unreliable, and HEC's draft rules out penalties based on a detector report alone. Design the process into the assessment instead: drafts, process evidence and a short oral check. (Slides: Action plan, redesign one assessment; appendix detection slides) **Fix in Curipod:** this slide still shows the old local-models explanation.

**8. LTI 1.3.** Your university wants to connect a third-party AI tutoring tool to Sakai so that student grades can flow back automatically and class rosters stay in sync. Which standard is the modern, more secure choice?
- A. ✅ LTI 1.3 / LTI Advantage
- B. LTI 1.1 (shared secret / OAuth 1.0a)
- C. SCORM 1.2
- D. I don't care

*Why:* LTI 1.3 replaces LTI 1.1's shared key and secret with signed tokens. Its LTI Advantage services handle grades and class lists. SCORM packages content; it doesn't connect to an external tool. In IBA's Sakai, an admin (CICT) registers LTI 1.3 tools once. (Slide: Into Sakai, three routes) **Fix in Curipod:** option A reads "LTI 1.3 / LTI" and option D "I dont care".

**9. Industry gap.** Recent 2025–2026 reports (WEF, LinkedIn, employer surveys) show a clear gap between what industry expects and what many university courses currently emphasise. The most accurate summary of that gap is:
- A. Industry no longer values technical skills; only soft skills matter.
- B. ✅ AI literacy and critical use of AI are essential skills for graduates.
- C. Universities are already producing exactly the AI-ready graduates industry wants.
- D. The only missing skill is coding in Python.

*Why:* The other three options either deny the gap or reduce it to one extreme. This question is not based on the Workshop Day Slides, so cite your own source if you quote figures. **Fix in Curipod:** this slide still shows the old Sakai-import explanation.

**10. IBA rules.** A student asks what IBA's rule on AI use is. Under HEC's August 2026 draft policy, where must IBA set out its AI rules?
- A. Only in HEC's national policy
- B. In Turnitin's AI settings
- C. Nowhere: AI rules are optional
- D. ✅ An IBA AI policy, and every course outline

*Why:* HEC's draft asks every university to write its own AI policy, set up an AI committee and report to HEC each year. Every course outline must also state what AI use is allowed. We found no public IBA-wide AI policy (searched Oct 2026), so for now students should follow each course outline. (Slide: Pakistan, national moves) **Check before the day:** if IBA has an internal AI policy that isn't public, update this explanation.

**11. NotebookLM.** Why is Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) better than a general chatbot for working from your own paper?
- A. It never makes mistakes
- B. It searches the whole web
- C. ✅ It answers from your uploads, with citations
- D. It uses a bigger model

*Why:* It works from your sources and cites them. Grounded doesn't mean correct, though: a citation can point to the right page and still misread it. (Slides: The research difference; Why this tool)
