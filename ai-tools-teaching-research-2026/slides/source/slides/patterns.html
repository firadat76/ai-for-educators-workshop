Four patterns repeat in every region. First, secure access: either licensed versions of the big assistants or a university or state-run portal, such as Yale’s Clarity, Amsterdam’s UvA AI Chat or North Rhine-Westphalia’s KI:connect.nrw, which state that prompts are not used to train models. Second, teacher-built course bots, where teachers ground an AI tutor in their own materials. Third, AI literacy: NUS makes AI courses compulsory for all undergraduates, and Oxford, LSE and Manchester pair their licences with training. Fourth, AI detection is being turned off, not added: Yale, Vanderbilt and Johns Hopkins have disabled Turnitin’s AI detector, and Manchester’s 2023 guidance called detectors unreliable and bars their output as evidence in summative work.
