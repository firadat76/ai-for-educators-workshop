Closer to home, the picture is different. None of the six universities checked publicly offers a campus-wide licence for ChatGPT Edu, Copilot, Gemini or Claude. What they do have is policy. LUMS has the most developed approach: the AI Policy Committee’s draft framework, “Guiding AI at LUMS,” has every course choose one of three policies: maximally restrictive, mixed (selective AI use), or AI-encouraged. The LUMS Learning Institute runs a monthly, faculty-focused AI Teaching Lab. FAST-NUCES sets rules in its 2026 student handbook: students are allowed, and sometimes encouraged, to use ChatGPT for assignments, and each assignment has a linked quiz or demo that carries most of the marks; screenshots are needed when AI output is quoted; AI is prohibited in labs unless the lab instructor allows it; and staff may use Turnitin’s AI score when they suspect misuse. NUST’s 2026 postgraduate handbook has a chapter of Generative AI Guidelines. Habib’s Digital Humanities course teaches students to work with AI, and on 19 October 2026 Habib signed an AI-in-pedagogy MoU with IBA and AKU. For Air University and KSBL we found no public student-facing AI policy; KSBL teaches prompt engineering with ChatGPT in executive education. Absence of a public page does not prove absence of a policy, so check with colleagues there before quoting.
